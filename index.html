01 · The macro turn

Money has a price again.

How to read it: after eight years pinned at or below zero (shaded), both Japanese rates are climbing. For real estate, this is the first time since the 1990s that cap rates have had real competition from bonds.

02 · Prices

Three decades of falling home prices have reversed.

How to read it: the climb since 2013 is the first sustained recovery since the bubble burst — but the gap between the nominal and inflation-adjusted lines shows part of the recent run is inflation, not just real appreciation. This is a recovery, not (yet) a mania.

In dollars, Japan is still on sale.

04 · The inbound engine

Foreign demand is back — and it moved.

How to read it: blue prefectures host more foreign hotel nights than before the pandemic; red ones haven't recovered. The story is the spread — recovery is not a Tokyo-only phenomenon, and several regional markets have doubled.

Method

Macro series are cached for six hours, market prices for thirty minutes; everything else recomputes in your browser on each visit. The destination screen ranks regional prefectures (metro areas excluded) with at least 500,000 foreign guest nights on three equally-weighted measures: growth in foreign nights since 2019, December–March resort-hotel occupancy, and the share of guests from long-haul markets.

A screening and monitoring tool — not investment advice. Property indices are national; individual markets vary widely.
